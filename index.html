The Florida Factor

Why Concrete Deteriorates

Florida's climate is among the most demanding in North America for concrete surfaces. Extreme humidity, intense UV radiation, seasonal heat cycles, and constant chemical exposure combine to silently destroy bare concrete — making a professional coating system not a luxury, but a necessity for any Jacksonville-area home or business.

01

Humidity & Moisture Intrusion

Florida averages over 70% relative humidity year-round. Bare concrete is porous and actively absorbs moisture from both the air and ground below. Over time this leads to mold growth, efflorescence, surface spalling, and structural breakdown. A sealed epoxy or polyaspartic coating creates a continuous moisture barrier — stopping intrusion at the surface before it reaches the slab.

02

Intense UV Exposure

Northeast Florida receives more than 230 sunny days per year, with one of the highest UV indexes in the continental U.S. Prolonged UV exposure erodes unprotected concrete — causing surface chalking, fading, and aggregate exposure. Polyaspartic systems are 100% UV-stable by formulation, maintaining gloss and color for 15–20 years without yellowing or hazing.

03

Heat & Thermal Stress

Jacksonville summer highs regularly exceed 95°F. The repeated cycle of thermal expansion during the day and contraction at night opens micro-fractures in bare concrete over time — allowing water, oil, and chemicals to penetrate deeper into the slab. Epoxy and polyaspartic coatings flex with the concrete through these cycles, sealing the surface and preventing progressive crack formation.

04

Oil & Chemical Damage

Motor oil, transmission fluid, battery acid, fertilizers, and cleaning chemicals permanently stain and chemically degrade bare concrete slabs. Once absorbed, these contaminants are impossible to fully remove and continue reacting with the concrete over time. A coated surface forms a chemical-resistant barrier — spills bead on top and wipe clean rather than penetrating the slab.

Protecting Your Investment

Replacing a concrete garage slab in Florida averages $6–$12 per square foot — a standard two-car garage can exceed $12,000 in replacement costs alone, not including the disruption to your home. A professional-grade coating system protects that existing concrete at a fraction of the cost, extending its functional life by 15 to 25 years.


In Jacksonville, Ponte Vedra, Nocatee, and St. Augustine — markets where home values are appreciating and buyers expect move-in quality — a finished, sealed garage floor signals pride of ownership. Real estate professionals consistently cite it as one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost improvements a homeowner can make before listing.

Common Questions

Have a question not answered here? Give us a call — we're happy to walk you through everything.

How long does an epoxy installation take? +
Most residential projects are completed in 1–3 days. Polyaspartic systems cure faster and can often be done in a single day. We'll give you an exact timeline during your free consultation.
Do you offer a warranty? +
Yes — all installations come with a 5-year workmanship warranty. Our products also carry manufacturer warranties. You're fully covered.
What areas do you serve? +
We serve the greater Jacksonville area including Jacksonville Beach, Ponte Vedra Beach, St. Augustine, Fleming Island, Orange Park, and surrounding communities.
How do I maintain my epoxy floor? +
Our coatings are extremely low-maintenance. Regular sweeping and occasional damp mopping with a pH-neutral cleaner is all that's needed. We provide a full care guide at completion.
Can epoxy go over existing concrete? +
In most cases, yes. We diamond-grind the surface to open the pores and ensure a permanent bond. Any cracks or damage are addressed during prep. We'll assess your slab at the free consultation.
